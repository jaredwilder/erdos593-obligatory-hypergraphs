1. Introduction 
 For an integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 a k k -uniform hypergraph is a pair H = ( V , E ) H=(V,E) consisting
of a (finite or infinite) set of vertices V V and a
set E ⊆ V ( k ) = { e ⊆ V : | e | = k } E\subseteq V^{(k)}=\{e\subseteq V\colon|e|=k\} of k k -element subsets
of V V , called the edges of H H .
The chromatic number of a hypergraph H = ( V , E ) H=(V,E) , denoted by χ ⁡ ( H ) \chi(H) , is the
least cardinal κ \kappa for which there exists a colouring f : V ⟶ κ f\colon V\longrightarrow\kappa such
that no edge of H H is monochromatic with respect to f f . A finite hypergraph F F is
said to be obligatory if every hypergraph H H with χ ⁡ ( H ) ≥ ℵ 1 \chi(H)\geq\aleph_{1} has
a subhypergraph isomorphic to F F .
Erdős and Hajnal [ 3 ] *Corollary 5.6 and Theorem 7.4 obtained the
following characterisation of obligatory graphs. 
 Theorem 1.1 (Erdős and Hajnal) . 
 A finite graph is obligatory if and only if it is bipartite. ∎ 
 For general hypergraphs no comparable result is known, and no plausible conjecture
has ever been proposed. The earliest contribution to this area we are aware
of is due to Erdős, Hajnal, and Rothschild [ 2 ] , who showed that
hypergraphs with two edges intersecting in two or more vertices are non-obligatory.
A systemstic study of the 3 3 -uniform case was initiated by
Erdős, Galvin, and Hajnal [ 1 ] , and later continued by
Komjáth [ 5 , 6 , 4 ] . Many results from [ 5 ] can be
extended in a straightforward manner to k k -uniform hypergraphs. For instance,
every obligatory k k -uniform hypergraph is k k -partite, and the class of obligatory,
 k k -uniform hypergraphs is closed under taking disjoint unions and one-point
amalgamations. In particular, every finite forest is obligatory, i.e., every finite
hypergraph F F whose set of edges admits an enumeration E ⁡ ( F ) = { e 0 , … , e n − 1 } E(F)=\{e_{0},\dots,e_{n-1}\} 
such that | e i ∩ ⋃ j < i e j | ≤ 1 \big|e_{i}\cap\bigcup_{j<i}e_{j}\big|\leq 1 holds for every positive i < n i<n .
To the best of our knowledge, however, it has never been shown that for any k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 
there exists an obligatory, k k -uniform hypergraph which fails to be a forest.

 Abstract. 
 Erdős and Hajnal proved that every graph of uncountable chromatic number
contains arbitrarily large finite, complete, bipartite graphs. We extend this
result to hypergraphs. 
 Key words and phrases: chromatic number, infinite hypergraphs
 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification 03E05, 05C15, 05D10
